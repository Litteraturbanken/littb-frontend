# AngularJS-to-Nuxt case-study microsite design

## Purpose

Build a Swedish-language, internal editorial microsite explaining why
Litteraturbanken replaced its AngularJS frontend with Nuxt and Vue, what the
rewrite changed, how GPT-5.6 Sol was used, how correctness is established, and
which risks remain.

The result is a scroll-based marketing-style case study, not a slide deck. It
must be candid, calm, evidence-led, and understandable to non-technical staff.
It must acknowledge the value of the old application before explaining why its
foundation became unsustainable.

The site is unlisted and marked `noindex, nofollow`. It has no authentication.
Anyone with the URL can open it; this is intentional security by obscurity, not
an access-control boundary.

## Scope and isolation

The case study is a standalone Sites project. It does not add a route to the
main Nuxt application and does not change Litteraturbanken's public website.
Its source, evidence data, measurement scripts, tests, raw artifacts, and
documentation live together in an isolated project surface.

The site has one public route with semantic anchored chapters. Methodology,
sources, raw measurement summaries, and limitations are available as in-page
appendices so the narrative remains continuous and directly linkable without
introducing application navigation.

No login, database, uploads, forms, analytics, or external runtime data are
required. The published site is a static evidence snapshot tied to recorded
Git revisions and measurement dates.

## Narrative

The page follows fourteen concise chapters:

1. **Varför vi gjorde det här** — the rewrite in one sentence.
2. **Vad den gamla webbplatsen åstadkom** — acknowledge its long service.
3. **När omvärlden gick vidare** — AngularJS support ended in January 2022 and
   the maintenance risk grew.
4. **Problemet med att inte synas** — demonstrate what a crawler receives
   before client JavaScript runs.
5. **Samma webbplats, ny motor** — explain the architectural change and the
   deliberate absence of a redesign.
6. **Varför Nuxt och Vue** — organizational familiarity, shared conventions,
   and hybrid rendering rather than framework popularity.
7. **Vad som faktiskt ändrades** — an accessible old/new architecture
   comparison.
8. **Vad som medvetet bevarades** — feature and visual parity as constraints.
9. **Resultat som går att mäta** — route-based performance and initial-HTML
   evidence with methodology close at hand.
10. **Arbetets omfattning** — categorized code, routes, tests, types, and
    migration scope.
11. **Hur AI användes** — GPT-5.6 Sol produced most implementation under human
    direction and made mistakes that comparison and testing exposed.
12. **Hur resultatet blir granskningsbart** — types, linting, tests, staging,
    Git, and ordinary maintainable artifacts.
13. **Från felrapport till överblick** — structured frontend/backend events,
    Vector, Grafana dashboards, and alerting.
14. **Det som återstår** — limitations, incomplete parity, and what the new
    foundation enables next without a victory lap.

## Visual direction

The design uses Litteraturbanken's restrained editorial identity: warm paper
tones, charcoal ink, muted burgundy, archival imagery, and large literary
typography. It should feel like a carefully edited long-form publication rather
than a software landing page or a dashboard.

The first viewport states the architectural change plainly and pairs an
AngularJS-era date with the current Nuxt revision. A narrow sticky chapter rail
shows reading progress on wide screens and becomes a compact chapter control on
small screens. Each chapter has one dominant idea and at most one supporting
interactive evidence block.

Purposeful interactions include:

- a before/after initial-HTML comparison;
- route tabs for performance measurements;
- mobile/desktop and legacy/Nuxt switches;
- a matched screenshot toggle when comparable captures exist;
- an accessible client-rendering versus hybrid-rendering diagram;
- a layered quality model;
- expandable methodology, sources, and limitations.

Motion explains state changes and reading progress only. All animation respects
`prefers-reduced-motion`; content remains complete and understandable without
motion or JavaScript. Model-authored SVG illustrations are not used. Existing
Litteraturbanken assets may be reused when their source and rights are clear;
otherwise typography, CSS shapes, and measured screenshots carry the design.

## Evidence contract

Every displayed claim has one of three labels in structured data:

- `external_fact`: supported by a cited authoritative source;
- `local_measurement`: generated by a checked-in reproducible script and linked
  to raw artifacts;
- `interpretation`: a clearly worded conclusion based on identified evidence.

The site may state that AngularJS support ended in January 2022, citing the
official AngularJS version-support page. It may describe general consequences
of unsupported foundational software in plain language, but it must not claim a
specific security incident without evidence.

The comparison freezes these candidate revisions:

- AngularJS frontend: `1f942453c31f8d9e5ae600fa7ec183e2603dcd46`;
- Nuxt frontend: `7511e5bd411db20abad11f69961775d0f0f71b86`;
- FastAPI backend: `d8a18ec1fca1a87a9a79e175f4ff2ed3d6a9a441`.

The AngularJS revision remains a candidate until its locally produced bundle is
matched to the deployed `red.litteraturbanken.se` asset fingerprint. The
deployment's `Last-Modified` timestamp aligns with the candidate commit, but
that timestamp alone is not sufficient proof. If bundle matching cannot prove
the revision, the site identifies the legacy side as a timestamped deployment
snapshot and explicitly says that its source commit is unverified.

Representative routes are:

| Surface | Path |
| --- | --- |
| Home | `/` |
| Library | `/bibliotek?filter=strindberg` |
| Text search | `/sök?fras=kyrka` |
| Author | `/författare/SöderbergH` |
| OCR reader | `/författare/SöderbergH/titlar/DoktorGlas/sida/1/etext` |
| Facsimile reader | `/författare/BoyeK/titlar/EttVerkligtJordiskt/sida/3/faksimil` |
| Editor | `/editor/lb11625223/ix/2/f` |

All routes must return comparable content on both deployments before they enter
a chart. A failing or materially different route is reported as a limitation,
not silently replaced after measurement.

## Measurements

Checked-in scripts produce raw artifacts and normalized structured data for:

- source lines split into application code, tests, styles, generated code,
  fixtures, configuration, and vendored code;
- initial HTML response bytes, meaningful text, links, title, description,
  canonical metadata, and behavior without JavaScript;
- Lighthouse performance, accessibility, SEO, and best-practice results;
- LCP, CLS, TBT, Speed Index, transferred bytes, JavaScript bytes, and request
  count where Lighthouse reports them reliably;
- response timing and useful-content presence;
- test counts grouped by lint, static typing, unit, SSR, Playwright,
  visual-regression, architecture, and API-contract checks;
- frontend route and feature-parity inventory;
- generated API types and typed client surface;
- observability components and verified event flow.

Performance runs use the same machine, Chromium version, viewport, emulation,
network settings, cold-cache policy, and route content. Mobile and desktop run
separately. Each route/configuration receives multiple samples; normalized data
uses the median and retains every raw report. The optimized Nuxt production
deployment is used, never the development server.

The deployed legacy and Nuxt sites currently use different backend
environments. End-to-end measurements therefore describe the deployments as a
whole and are not presented as isolated framework benchmarks. A controlled
same-backend comparison is included only if it can be reproduced without
modifying application behavior.

## Claims intentionally excluded or qualified

The microsite does not claim:

- historical pre-rewrite performance from a measurement made today;
- real-user improvement without comparable field data;
- framework-only speed gains from different end-to-end environments;
- a security score derived from incomparable dependency audits;
- a percentage for type coverage without a reproducible definition;
- complete visual parity from a small screenshot sample;
- that test count alone establishes quality;
- that AI-generated code is inherently safe or reliable;
- that every migrated feature is complete.

Noisy or inconclusive results remain visible in the methodology and limitation
data, even when omitted from the main narrative.

## Data and component boundaries

Content and evidence are not hard-coded throughout the page. The project keeps
small typed modules or JSON files for:

- chapters and editorial copy;
- claims and citations;
- route identities;
- frozen revisions and deployment metadata;
- normalized benchmark results;
- test and code inventories;
- parity status;
- known limitations.

Presentation components consume those data structures:

- `ChapterNav` owns anchor navigation and progress;
- `EvidenceCard` renders claim type, value, date, and source;
- `InitialHtmlCompare` renders the crawler-view comparison;
- `PerformanceCompare` renders route/device medians and raw-report links;
- `ArchitectureStory` explains client rendering and hybrid rendering;
- `ParityMatrix` distinguishes verified, partial, and deferred behavior;
- `QualityLayers` presents human direction, types, linting, tests, staging,
  observability, and Git;
- `MethodologyAppendix`, `SourcesAppendix`, and `LimitationsAppendix` expose the
  audit trail.

Components receive complete typed data and contain no network requests. Build
failure is preferred over silently rendering malformed evidence.

## Failure and honesty behavior

Missing required evidence fails normalization or the production build. Optional
metrics may render an explicit `Ej tillförlitligt mätbart` state with the reason
recorded in the data. The UI never substitutes a zero, hides a failed route, or
reuses a value from another configuration.

External citations are stored with title, publisher, URL, access date, and the
claim identifiers they support. Broken citation links fail a verification
script when network checking is enabled; offline builds retain the last
verified access metadata.

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

The document follows semantic heading order and provides a skip link. Anchored
chapters have stable IDs and visible focus. All toggles use buttons or native
controls with programmatic names and state. Comparisons do not rely on color
alone. Charts have text summaries and structured tables. Keyboard, touch, and
screen-reader users receive the same evidence.

At projector and desktop widths, the chapter rail and evidence can sit beside
the narrative. On tablets and phones, the page becomes a single reading column
and comparison controls wrap without horizontal scrolling. Print CSS removes
sticky controls and interaction chrome, expands details, prints source URLs,
and preserves a coherent report.

## Testing and verification

Automated checks cover:

- data-schema validation and claim/source linkage;
- chapter ordering, stable anchors, and direct links;
- keyboard-accessible controls and focus behavior;
- initial-HTML, performance, parity, and limitation rendering;
- reduced-motion behavior;
- no-index metadata and absence of accidental main-site links;
- responsive rendering at phone, desktop, and projector-like resolutions;
- print layout basics;
- production build compatibility with Sites.

The final verification includes a production build, automated tests, desktop
and projector screenshots, a visual review report, a link/source check, and a
fresh inspection of the published deployment. Raw measurement reports and the
normalization output are retained alongside the methodology document.

## Deliverables

The standalone Sites project contains:

1. the completed Swedish case-study microsite;
2. reproducible measurement scripts;
3. raw and normalized measurement data;
4. a methodology document;
5. authoritative external sources;
6. automated tests;
7. a short editorial guide explaining the intended reading sequence;
8. desktop, projector, and mobile verification captures or a visual report;
9. an unlisted, no-index Sites deployment URL.
